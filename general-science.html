1. Physics Basics

  • Physics is the study of matter, energy, motion and forces.
  • SI unit of length = metre (m).
  • SI unit of mass = kilogram (kg).
  • SI unit of time = second (s).
  • SI unit of force = Newton (N).
  • SI unit of energy = Joule (J).
  • SI unit of power = Watt (W).
  • SI unit of pressure = Pascal (Pa).
  • Speed = Distance ÷ Time.
  • Force = Mass × Acceleration.
  • Work = Force × Displacement.
  • Power = Work ÷ Time.

2. Motion and Force

  • Newton gave the three laws of motion.
  • First law is also called the law of inertia.
  • Second law relates force, mass and acceleration.
  • Third law states that every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
  • Gravity is the force by which Earth attracts objects.
  • Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.
  • Friction opposes motion between surfaces.

3. Heat and Temperature

  • Heat is a form of energy.
  • Temperature measures the degree of hotness or coldness.
  • SI unit of temperature = Kelvin.
  • Heat transfer occurs through conduction, convection and radiation.
  • Metals are generally good conductors of heat.
  • Radiation does not require a material medium.

4. Light

  • Light travels in a straight line in a uniform medium.
  • Speed of light in vacuum is approximately 3 × 10⁸ m/s.
  • Reflection is the bouncing back of light.
  • Refraction is the bending of light when it passes from one medium to another.
  • A convex lens can converge light rays.
  • A concave lens diverges light rays.
  • Rainbow is produced due to dispersion of sunlight.

5. Electricity

  • Electric current is the flow of electric charge.
  • SI unit of current = Ampere.
  • SI unit of resistance = Ohm.
  • SI unit of electric charge = Coulomb.
  • Voltage is measured in Volt.
  • Ohm's Law: V = IR.
  • Electric power = Voltage × Current.

6. Chemistry Basics

  • Chemistry is the study of matter and its changes.
  • Smallest unit of an element is an atom.
  • Two or more atoms chemically combined form a molecule.
  • Water formula = H₂O.
  • Carbon dioxide formula = CO₂.
  • Oxygen formula = O₂.
  • Nitrogen formula = N₂.
  • Common salt = Sodium Chloride (NaCl).

7. Acids, Bases and Salts

  • Acids generally have pH less than 7.
  • Bases generally have pH greater than 7.
  • Neutral substances have pH approximately 7.
  • Hydrochloric acid formula = HCl.
  • Sulphuric acid formula = H₂SO₄.
  • Sodium hydroxide formula = NaOH.
  • Acid + Base → Salt + Water.
  • Baking soda = Sodium bicarbonate.

8. Metals and Non-Metals

  • Iron, copper, aluminium and gold are metals.
  • Oxygen, nitrogen, sulphur and chlorine are non-metals.
  • Metals are generally good conductors of electricity.
  • Copper is widely used in electrical wires.
  • Mercury is a metal that is liquid at room temperature.
  • Graphite is a form of carbon that conducts electricity.

9. Biology Basics

  • Biology is the study of living organisms.
  • Cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
  • Robert Hooke discovered cells in cork.
  • Plants prepare food through photosynthesis.
  • Chlorophyll gives plants their green colour.
  • Photosynthesis uses carbon dioxide and water in the presence of sunlight.
  • Oxygen is released during photosynthesis.

10. Human Body

  • Heart pumps blood throughout the body.
  • Normal adult human heart has four chambers.
  • Brain controls many activities of the body.
  • Lungs are the main organs of respiration.
  • Kidneys filter waste from the blood.
  • Liver is one of the largest internal organs.
  • Red blood cells carry oxygen.
  • White blood cells help fight infections.
  • Platelets help in blood clotting.

11. Human Blood

  • Blood consists of plasma, red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets.
  • Haemoglobin gives red blood cells their red colour.
  • Haemoglobin carries oxygen.
  • Blood group system includes A, B, AB and O.
  • O negative is commonly known as the universal red-cell donor type.
  • AB positive is commonly known as the universal red-cell recipient type.

12. Vitamins

  • Vitamin A helps maintain normal vision.
  • Vitamin B group is important for metabolism and nervous-system functions.
  • Vitamin C is important for normal connective tissue and immune function.
  • Vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium.
  • Vitamin E acts as an antioxidant.
  • Vitamin K is important for blood clotting.

13. Diseases

  • Malaria is caused by Plasmodium parasites and transmitted by infected female Anopheles mosquitoes.
  • Dengue is caused by dengue viruses and is mainly spread by Aedes mosquitoes.
  • Tuberculosis is caused by bacteria.
  • Typhoid is caused by Salmonella Typhi bacteria.
  • Rabies is caused by a virus.
  • Polio is caused by poliovirus.

14. Plants

  • Roots absorb water and minerals from soil.
  • Leaves are major sites of photosynthesis.
  • Xylem transports water and minerals.
  • Phloem transports food made by plants.
  • Stomata help in gas exchange.
  • Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from plant surfaces, mainly through stomata.

15. Environment

  • Environment includes living and non-living components.
  • Food chain shows the transfer of energy between organisms.
  • Producers generally make their own food.
  • Consumers depend on other organisms for food.
  • Decomposers break down dead organic matter.
  • Carbon dioxide is a major greenhouse gas.
  • Ozone in the stratosphere helps protect Earth from harmful ultraviolet radiation.

16. Scientific Instruments

  • Thermometer measures temperature.
  • Barometer measures atmospheric pressure.
  • Hygrometer measures humidity.
  • Seismograph records ground vibrations from earthquakes.
  • Microscope is used to observe very small objects.
  • Telescope is used to observe distant objects.
  • Ammeter measures electric current.
  • Voltmeter measures potential difference.

17. Important Scientific Facts

  • Earth revolves around the Sun.
  • Earth rotates on its axis.
  • The Moon is Earth's natural satellite.
  • The Sun is a star.
  • Sound needs a medium to travel.
  • Sound cannot travel through vacuum.
  • Vaccines help train the immune system to respond to specific infections.
  • DNA carries genetic information in living organisms.
