1. Arrival of Europeans in India

European powers came to India mainly for trade and commercial purposes. The Portuguese were the first among the major European powers to establish a strong presence in India.

  • Portuguese: Vasco da Gama reached Calicut in 1498.
  • Dutch: Established trading centres in India.
  • English: English East India Company was established in 1600.
  • French: Established trading settlements including Pondicherry.
Exam Point: Vasco da Gama reached Calicut in 1498.

2. Battle of Plassey - 1757

The Battle of Plassey was fought on 23 June 1757 between the forces of Siraj-ud-daulah and the British East India Company led by Robert Clive.

  • Year: 1757
  • British commander: Robert Clive
  • Nawab of Bengal: Siraj-ud-daulah
  • British victory strengthened Company rule in Bengal.
Exam Point: Battle of Plassey is considered a major turning point in British political power in India.

3. Battle of Buxar - 1764

The Battle of Buxar was fought in 1764 between the British East India Company and the combined forces of Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-daulah and Shah Alam II.

  • Year: 1764
  • British commander: Hector Munro
  • British victory increased Company authority in eastern India.
  • Treaty of Allahabad followed in 1765.

4. Important British Acts

Regulating Act - 1773

The Regulating Act was the first major attempt by the British Parliament to regulate the affairs of the East India Company.

Pitt's India Act - 1784

Pitt's India Act established a system of dual control over the Company's political and commercial affairs.

Government of India Act - 1858

After the Revolt of 1857, the administration of India was transferred from the East India Company to the British Crown.

5. Social and Religious Reform Movements

  • Raja Ram Mohan Roy: Associated with Brahmo Samaj.
  • Brahmo Samaj: Founded in 1828.
  • Swami Dayanand Saraswati: Founded Arya Samaj in 1875.
  • Swami Vivekananda: Founded Ramakrishna Mission in 1897.
  • Sir Syed Ahmad Khan: Associated with the Aligarh Movement.
  • Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar: Supported widow remarriage and social reform.

6. Revolt of 1857

The Revolt of 1857 was a major uprising against British rule. It began at Meerut on 10 May 1857 and spread to several parts of northern and central India.

  • Immediate centre of outbreak: Meerut
  • Major centre: Delhi
  • Bahadur Shah II became the symbolic leader.
  • Rani Lakshmibai led resistance in Jhansi.
  • Nana Sahib was associated with Kanpur.
  • Begum Hazrat Mahal was associated with Lucknow.
  • Kunwar Singh was associated with Bihar.
Exam Point: The Revolt of 1857 is also known as the First War of Independence in many Indian historical accounts.

7. Indian National Congress

The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885. Allan Octavian Hume played an important role in its formation.

  • Founded: 1885
  • First session: Bombay
  • First President: W. C. Bonnerjee
  • First session was held in December 1885.

8. Partition of Bengal - 1905

Lord Curzon announced the partition of Bengal in 1905. The decision generated strong opposition and contributed to the Swadeshi Movement.

  • Partition announced: 1905
  • Viceroy: Lord Curzon
  • Swadeshi Movement became an important response.
  • Partition was annulled in 1911.

9. Gandhian Era

Champaran Satyagraha - 1917

Gandhi's first major satyagraha in India was associated with Champaran in Bihar.

Kheda Satyagraha - 1918

Gandhi supported peasants in Kheda, Gujarat, in their demand for relief from land revenue.

Non-Cooperation Movement - 1920

Gandhi launched the Non-Cooperation Movement in 1920. It was withdrawn in 1922 after the Chauri Chaura incident.

Civil Disobedience Movement - 1930

The movement began with the Salt March from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi.

Quit India Movement - 1942

The Quit India Movement was launched in August 1942 with the call for the British to leave India.

11. Subhas Chandra Bose and INA

Subhas Chandra Bose played an important role in India's freedom struggle. He became associated with the Indian National Army (INA).

  • Popularly known as Netaji.
  • Associated with the Indian National Army.
  • Gave the famous call "Give me blood, and I will give you freedom."
  • Formed the Provisional Government of Free India in 1943.

  • Rowlatt Act - 1919
  • Jallianwala Bagh Massacre - 1919
  • Non-Cooperation Movement - 1920

13. Jallianwala Bagh Massacre

The Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place at Amritsar on 13 April 1919. British troops under General Reginald Dyer opened fire on a large gathering.

Exam Point: Jallianwala Bagh is located in Amritsar, Punjab.

14. Simon Commission

The Simon Commission arrived in India in 1928. It was opposed because it did not include any Indian member.

Important: Lala Lajpat Rai led a protest against the Simon Commission in Lahore.

15. Indian Independence - 1947

India became independent on 15 August 1947. The Indian Independence Act was passed by the British Parliament in 1947.

  • Independence: 15 August 1947
  • First Prime Minister: Jawaharlal Nehru
  • First Governor-General of independent India: Lord Mountbatten
  • First Indian Governor-General: C. Rajagopalachari
