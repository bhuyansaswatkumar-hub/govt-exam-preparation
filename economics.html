Indian Economy Notes

SSC • Banking • Railway • Competitive Exams

1. What is Economics?

Economics is the study of how individuals, businesses and governments use limited resources to satisfy unlimited wants.

  • Microeconomics: Studies individuals, firms and individual markets.
  • Macroeconomics: Studies the economy as a whole.
  • Production: Creation of goods and services.
  • Consumption: Use of goods and services.
  • Distribution: Allocation of income and resources.

2. Indian Economy

India is a mixed economy where both public and private sectors operate.

  • Public Sector – Government owned enterprises.
  • Private Sector – Privately owned businesses.
  • Agriculture, industry and services are the major sectors.
  • The service sector has a major contribution to India's economic activity.

3. GDP and GNP

  • GDP: Value of final goods and services produced within a country's borders during a specific period.
  • GNP: GDP plus net factor income from abroad.
  • Nominal GDP: GDP measured at current prices.
  • Real GDP: GDP measured using constant prices.
  • Per Capita Income: National income divided by population.

4. Inflation

Inflation means a sustained rise in the general price level of goods and services.

  • CPI: Consumer Price Index.
  • WPI: Wholesale Price Index.
  • Demand-pull inflation: Inflation caused by excess demand.
  • Cost-push inflation: Inflation caused by rising production costs.
  • Deflation: A sustained fall in the general price level.

5. Monetary Policy

Monetary policy is used by the Reserve Bank of India to influence money supply, interest rates and credit conditions.

  • Repo Rate: Rate at which RBI lends short-term funds to banks against eligible securities.
  • Reverse Repo Rate: Rate at which RBI borrows funds from banks.
  • CRR: Cash Reserve Ratio.
  • SLR: Statutory Liquidity Ratio.
  • Bank Rate: Rate at which RBI lends to banks under the relevant facility.

6. Fiscal Policy

Fiscal policy relates to government taxation, expenditure and borrowing.

  • Government Revenue
  • Government Expenditure
  • Public Borrowing
  • Budget Deficit
  • Fiscal Deficit

7. Government Budget

The Union Budget presents the government's estimated receipts and expenditure for a financial year.

  • Revenue Receipts: Tax and non-tax receipts that do not create liabilities.
  • Capital Receipts: Receipts that create liabilities or reduce assets.
  • Revenue Expenditure: Expenditure generally not creating physical assets.
  • Capital Expenditure: Expenditure that creates assets or reduces liabilities.

8. Banking System

  • RBI: India's central bank.
  • Commercial Banks: Accept deposits and provide loans.
  • Regional Rural Banks: Focus on rural and agricultural areas.
  • Cooperative Banks: Operate on cooperative principles.
  • Scheduled Banks: Banks included in the Second Schedule of the RBI Act, subject to statutory criteria.

9. Reserve Bank of India

  • RBI is India's central bank.
  • RBI was established in 1935.
  • RBI was nationalised in 1949.
  • RBI manages monetary policy.
  • RBI regulates the banking system.
  • RBI manages foreign exchange reserves and currency issuance functions.

10. Taxes

  • Direct Tax: Tax burden is generally borne directly by the taxpayer.
  • Indirect Tax: Tax can be passed on through prices.
  • Income Tax: Direct tax on taxable income.
  • GST: Goods and Services Tax.
  • Corporate Tax: Tax on corporate income.

11. GST

GST is a destination-based indirect tax on the supply of goods and services. It was introduced in India on 1 July 2017.

  • CGST – Central Goods and Services Tax
  • SGST – State Goods and Services Tax
  • IGST – Integrated Goods and Services Tax
  • GST is based on the principle of input tax credit.

12. National Income

  • National income measures income generated by an economy over a period.
  • GDP measures domestic production.
  • GNP measures production attributable to a country's residents, subject to the accounting definition used.
  • Per capita income is used as an indicator of average income.

13. Unemployment

Unemployment occurs when people who are willing and able to work cannot find suitable employment.

  • Seasonal Unemployment: Employment available only during certain seasons.
  • Disguised Unemployment: More workers are employed than required.
  • Structural Unemployment: Mismatch between skills and available jobs.
  • Frictional Unemployment: Temporary unemployment while changing jobs.

14. Poverty

Poverty refers to a condition where people lack sufficient resources to meet basic needs.

  • Food and nutrition
  • Housing
  • Education
  • Healthcare
  • Employment opportunities

15. Planning in India

  • India adopted economic planning after independence.
  • The Planning Commission was established in 1950.
  • Five-Year Plans were used as a major planning framework.
  • NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission in 2015.

16. NITI Aayog

  • Full form: National Institution for Transforming India.
  • Established in 2015.
  • It replaced the Planning Commission.
  • It promotes cooperative and competitive federalism.
  • It acts as a policy think tank of the Government of India.

17. Agriculture

  • Agriculture is an important sector of the Indian economy.
  • Major food crops include rice and wheat.
  • Cash crops include cotton, sugarcane, tea and coffee.
  • Green Revolution significantly increased food-grain production.

18. Green Revolution

  • Associated with increased agricultural productivity.
  • Use of high-yielding variety seeds played an important role.
  • Irrigation, fertilisers and improved farming practices were important factors.
  • Wheat production benefited significantly in the early phase.

19. Balance of Payments

Balance of Payments records the economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world during a period.

  • Current Account
  • Capital/Financial Account
  • Foreign Exchange Transactions

20. Important Economic Terms

  • Repo: Short-term borrowing mechanism involving RBI and banks.
  • Liquidity: Ease with which an asset can be converted into cash.
  • Fiscal Deficit: Excess of total expenditure over total receipts excluding debt receipts.
  • Revenue Deficit: Excess of revenue expenditure over revenue receipts.
  • Subsidy: Financial assistance provided by government to support particular activities or groups.
  • Disinvestment: Sale of government stake in a public sector enterprise.
