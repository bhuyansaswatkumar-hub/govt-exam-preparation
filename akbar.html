Akbar - Important Notes

Akbar was the third Mughal emperor of India. He is considered one of the most important rulers of medieval Indian history. His reign is especially important for SSC, Banking, Railway and other competitive examinations.

Born: 15 October 1542
Father: Humayun
Mother: Hamida Banu Begum
Reign: 1556–1605
Dynasty: Mughal Dynasty
Capital: Agra; later Fatehpur Sikri

1. Early Life

  • Akbar was born at Umarkot in Sindh.
  • His father was Humayun, the second Mughal emperor.
  • His mother was Hamida Banu Begum.
  • Akbar became emperor in 1556 after the death of Humayun.
  • At the time of his accession, Akbar was still a teenager.
  • Bairam Khan acted as Akbar's regent during the early years.

2. Second Battle of Panipat

The Second Battle of Panipat was fought in 1556 between the forces of Akbar and Hemu.

Year: 1556
Akbar's side: Mughal forces
Opponent: Hemu
Result: Mughal victory

The victory helped Akbar establish Mughal control over northern India.

3. Akbar's Expansion

  • Akbar expanded Mughal power across large parts of India.
  • He followed a combination of military expansion and diplomacy.
  • Several Rajput states accepted Mughal authority.
  • Gujarat was conquered in 1572–73.
  • Bengal was brought under Mughal control in the 1570s.
  • Akbar also expanded towards Rajasthan and the Deccan.

4. Rajput Policy

Akbar is known for developing alliances with several Rajput rulers and nobles.

  • He formed political alliances with several Rajput states.
  • Rajput nobles were given important positions in the Mughal administration.
  • Akbar followed a relatively broad policy of religious tolerance.
  • Mewar continued to resist Mughal expansion for a long period.

5. Religious Policy

  • Akbar abolished the pilgrimage tax in 1563.
  • He abolished the jizya tax in 1564.
  • He encouraged discussions between followers of different religions.
  • Ibadat Khana was established at Fatehpur Sikri.
  • Akbar promoted the idea of Sulh-i-Kul, meaning universal peace.

6. Din-i-Ilahi

In 1582, Akbar introduced an ethical and spiritual order commonly known as Din-i-Ilahi.

Important: Din-i-Ilahi was not a mass religion. It had very few followers and was closely associated with Akbar's personal spiritual ideas.

7. Akbar's Administration

Mansabdari System

Akbar developed the Mansabdari system to organize the Mughal administrative and military structure.

  • Officials were known as Mansabdars.
  • Mansabs indicated the rank of officials.
  • The system helped organize military and administrative service.

Revenue System

  • Raja Todar Mal was associated with Akbar's revenue administration.
  • The Dahsala system was developed during Akbar's reign.
  • Land revenue assessment was an important part of Mughal administration.

8. Navratnas of Akbar

Akbar's court was associated with several famous scholars, administrators, artists and intellectuals, traditionally remembered as the Navratnas.

  • Birbal
  • Raja Todar Mal
  • Abul Fazl
  • Faizi
  • Raja Man Singh
  • Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khana
  • Tansen
  • Fakir Aziao-Din
  • Mulla Do-Piyaza

9. Important Buildings

  • Fatehpur Sikri was developed as an important imperial city.
  • Buland Darwaza was constructed during Akbar's reign.
  • Akbar's tomb is located at Sikandra near Agra.
  • Akbar's architectural style combined different cultural influences.

10. Important Dates

1542: Birth of Akbar
1556: Akbar became Mughal emperor
1556: Second Battle of Panipat
1563: Pilgrimage tax abolished
1564: Jizya abolished
1572–73: Gujarat campaign
1582: Din-i-Ilahi associated with Akbar
1605: Death of Akbar
