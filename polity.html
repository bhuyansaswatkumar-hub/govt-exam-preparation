1. Indian Constitution – Basic Facts

  • The Constitution of India was adopted on 26 November 1949.
  • The Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950.
  • 26 November is observed as Constitution Day.
  • India is described as a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic.
  • The Constitution provides a parliamentary form of government.
  • India has a federal system with a strong unitary bias.
  • Dr. B. R. Ambedkar was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee.
  • Dr. Rajendra Prasad was the President of the Constituent Assembly.
  • B. N. Rau was the Constitutional Adviser to the Constituent Assembly.

2. Preamble

The Preamble begins with the words "We, the People of India".

Important objectives

  • Justice – Social, Economic and Political
  • Liberty – Thought, Expression, Belief, Faith and Worship
  • Equality – Status and Opportunity
  • Fraternity – Dignity of the individual and unity and integrity of the nation

The words Socialist, Secular and Integrity were added by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976.

3. Fundamental Rights

Fundamental Rights are contained in Part III of the Constitution.

  • Article 14–18: Right to Equality
  • Article 19–22: Right to Freedom
  • Article 23–24: Right against Exploitation
  • Article 25–28: Right to Freedom of Religion
  • Article 29–30: Cultural and Educational Rights
  • Article 32: Right to Constitutional Remedies

Dr. B. R. Ambedkar called Article 32 the "heart and soul" of the Constitution.

The Right to Property was removed from the list of Fundamental Rights by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978. It is now a constitutional/legal right under Article 300A.

4. Directive Principles of State Policy

  • DPSPs are contained in Part IV.
  • Articles 36–51 deal with DPSPs.
  • They are not enforceable by courts.
  • They aim to establish social and economic democracy.
  • The idea was borrowed mainly from the Irish Constitution.

5. Fundamental Duties

  • Fundamental Duties are contained in Article 51A.
  • They were added by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976.
  • Originally there were 10 Fundamental Duties.
  • The 86th Amendment Act, 2002 added the 11th duty.
  • There are currently 11 Fundamental Duties.

6. President of India

  • The President is the constitutional head of India.
  • Article 52 provides for the office of the President.
  • The President is elected indirectly.
  • The term of office is 5 years.
  • The minimum age for becoming President is 35 years.
  • The President can be removed through impeachment.
  • The President is the Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces.

7. Vice-President

  • Article 63 provides for the Vice-President.
  • The Vice-President is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
  • The term is 5 years.
  • The minimum age is 35 years.
  • The Vice-President is elected by members of both Houses of Parliament.

8. Prime Minister and Council of Ministers

  • The Prime Minister is the real executive head of the Union government.
  • The Prime Minister is appointed by the President.
  • The Council of Ministers is headed by the Prime Minister.
  • The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
  • The Prime Minister acts as a link between the President and the Council of Ministers.

9. Parliament of India

Parliament consists of:

  • The President
  • Rajya Sabha
  • Lok Sabha

Rajya Sabha

  • It is the Upper House.
  • It is a permanent House.
  • It cannot be dissolved.
  • One-third of its members retire every two years.
  • A member normally has a six-year term.

Lok Sabha

  • It is the Lower House.
  • Its normal term is five years.
  • It can be dissolved before completion of its term.
  • The minimum age for Lok Sabha membership is 25 years.

10. Supreme Court

  • The Supreme Court is the highest court of India.
  • It was established on 28 January 1950.
  • Article 124 deals with the Supreme Court.
  • The Chief Justice of India heads the Supreme Court.
  • Judges of the Supreme Court retire at the age of 65 years.
  • The Supreme Court has original, appellate and advisory jurisdiction.

11. High Courts

  • High Courts are the highest courts at the state level.
  • Article 214 provides for High Courts for states.
  • High Court judges retire at the age of 62 years.
  • The High Court can issue writs for enforcement of Fundamental Rights and for other purposes within its jurisdiction.

12. Governor

  • The Governor is the constitutional head of a state.
  • The Governor is appointed by the President.
  • The normal term is 5 years, subject to the pleasure of the President.
  • The minimum age is 35 years.
  • The Governor has executive, legislative and discretionary powers.

13. Chief Minister

  • The Chief Minister is the real executive head of a state.
  • The Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor.
  • The Council of Ministers is headed by the Chief Minister.
  • The state Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly.

14. Constitutional Bodies

  • Election Commission of India: Article 324
  • UPSC: Article 315
  • Finance Commission: Article 280
  • Comptroller and Auditor General: Article 148
  • Attorney General of India: Article 76
  • Advocate General of State: Article 165

15. Emergency Provisions

National Emergency

Article 352. It may be declared on grounds of war, external aggression or armed rebellion.

President's Rule

Article 356. It is imposed when the constitutional machinery of a state fails.

Financial Emergency

Article 360. It may be declared when the financial stability or credit of India or any part of it is threatened.

16. Important Constitutional Amendments

  • 42nd Amendment, 1976: Known as the Mini Constitution.
  • 44th Amendment, 1978: Right to Property removed from Fundamental Rights.
  • 52nd Amendment, 1985: Anti-defection provisions.
  • 61st Amendment, 1988: Voting age reduced from 21 to 18 years.
  • 73rd Amendment, 1992: Panchayati Raj institutions.
  • 74th Amendment, 1992: Urban local bodies.
  • 86th Amendment, 2002: Right to Education under Article 21A.
  • 101st Amendment, 2016: GST.

17. Panchayati Raj

  • The 73rd Constitutional Amendment gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj institutions.
  • It added Part IX to the Constitution.
  • It also added the 11th Schedule.
  • The 11th Schedule contains 29 subjects.
  • The Gram Sabha is an important institution of local self-government.

18. Municipalities

  • The 74th Constitutional Amendment gave constitutional status to municipalities.
  • It added Part IX-A.
  • It added the 12th Schedule.
  • The 12th Schedule contains 18 subjects.

19. Important Articles

  • Article 1 – India, that is Bharat
  • Article 14 – Equality before law
  • Article 19 – Six freedoms
  • Article 21 – Protection of life and personal liberty
  • Article 21A – Right to Education
  • Article 32 – Constitutional remedies
  • Article 44 – Uniform Civil Code
  • Article 51A – Fundamental Duties
  • Article 52 – President
  • Article 63 – Vice-President
  • Article 76 – Attorney General
  • Article 123 – President's Ordinance power
  • Article 143 – Advisory jurisdiction of Supreme Court
  • Article 148 – CAG
  • Article 280 – Finance Commission
  • Article 324 – Election Commission
  • Article 352 – National Emergency
  • Article 356 – President's Rule
  • Article 360 – Financial Emergency
