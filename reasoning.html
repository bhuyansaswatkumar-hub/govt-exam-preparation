Reasoning Notes

SSC • Banking • Competitive Exams

📚 Reasoning Preparation

These notes cover important reasoning topics frequently asked in SSC, Banking and other competitive examinations.

1. Analogy

Analogy questions test the relationship between two objects, numbers, letters or words.

Example

Book : Reading :: Pen : ?

Answer: Writing

Common analogy types:

  • Word Analogy
  • Number Analogy
  • Letter Analogy
  • Meaning-based Analogy

2. Classification

Classification questions ask you to identify the item that is different from the others.

Example

Apple, Mango, Banana, Potato

Answer: Potato — it is a vegetable while the others are fruits.

3. Number Series

In number series, identify the pattern and find the missing number.

Example

2, 4, 8, 16, ?

Answer: 32

Common patterns include:

  • Addition
  • Subtraction
  • Multiplication
  • Division
  • Squares
  • Cubes

4. Alphabet Series

Alphabet series questions use positions of letters in the English alphabet.

Letter Position
A1
B2
C3
D4
Z26

5. Coding-Decoding

Coding-decoding questions require finding the rule used to convert one word or number into another.

Example

If CAT is coded as DBU, then DOG is coded as?

C → D
A → B
T → U

Therefore:

D → E
O → P
G → H

Answer: EPH

6. Blood Relations

Blood relation questions test family relationships.

Important Relations

  • Father's son = Brother / Self
  • Father's daughter = Sister / Self
  • Mother's son = Brother / Self
  • Mother's daughter = Sister / Self
  • Brother's son = Nephew
  • Brother's daughter = Niece
  • Sister's son = Nephew
  • Sister's daughter = Niece
  • Father's father = Grandfather
  • Mother's mother = Grandmother

7. Direction Sense

The four main directions are:

  • North
  • South
  • East
  • West

Opposite directions:

  • North ↔ South
  • East ↔ West

Diagonal directions:

  • North-East
  • North-West
  • South-East
  • South-West

8. Syllogism

Syllogism questions contain statements followed by conclusions. You must determine whether the conclusions logically follow.

Example

Statement:

All cats are animals.

Some animals are black.

The conclusion must be checked using logical relationships rather than general knowledge.

9. Ranking Test

Ranking questions ask about the position of a person from the beginning or from the end.

Important Formula

Total persons = Rank from beginning + Rank from end − 1

Example

A student is 10th from the top and 15th from the bottom.

Total = 10 + 15 − 1 = 24

10. Calendar

Important facts:

  • Ordinary year = 365 days
  • Leap year = 366 days
  • Ordinary year has 1 odd day
  • Leap year has 2 odd days

A leap year is generally divisible by 4, but century years must also be divisible by 400.

Example: 2000 was a leap year, while 1900 was not.

11. Clock

Important facts:

  • Minute hand moves 6° per minute.
  • Hour hand moves 0.5° per minute.
  • One complete circle = 360°.

Angle Formula

Angle = |30H − 5.5M|

Where H = hour and M = minutes.

12. Seating Arrangement

Seating arrangement questions involve placing people according to given conditions.

Common types:

  • Linear Arrangement
  • Circular Arrangement
  • Square Arrangement
  • Rectangular Arrangement

Always draw a diagram before solving complex arrangement questions.

13. Venn Diagram

Venn diagrams represent relationships between different groups.

Example

Students who play Cricket, Football and both can be represented using overlapping circles.

14. Statement and Conclusion

Read the statement carefully and determine whether the conclusion logically follows from the information given.

Do not use outside information unless the question specifically requires it.

15. Important Reasoning Tips

  • Read the question carefully.
  • Identify the pattern.
  • Use diagrams whenever possible.
  • Write down important information.
  • Eliminate impossible options.
  • Practice questions regularly.
  • Improve calculation speed.
  • Review mistakes after every test.
