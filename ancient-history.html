📚 Ancient Indian History - Quick Facts

  • Indus Valley Civilization: c. 2600–1900 BCE
  • Great Bath: Mohenjo-daro
  • Oldest Veda: Rigveda
  • Mahavira: 24th Tirthankara of Jainism
  • Gautama Buddha: Founder of Buddhism
  • First Buddhist Council: Rajgir
  • Founder of Mauryan Empire: Chandragupta Maurya
  • Arthashastra: Kautilya
  • Ashoka: Mauryan ruler who embraced Buddhism after Kalinga War
  • Gupta Period: Known for achievements in art, science and literature
  • Kalidasa: Famous Sanskrit poet and dramatist
  • Ajanta Caves: Famous for Buddhist paintings

1. Indus Valley Civilization

The Indus Valley Civilization was one of the earliest urban civilizations of the Indian subcontinent. Important sites have been discovered in present-day India and Pakistan.

  • Harappa: Located in present-day Punjab, Pakistan.
  • Mohenjo-daro: Located in Sindh, Pakistan.
  • Dholavira: Located in Gujarat, India.
  • Lothal: Located in Gujarat and associated with a dockyard.
  • Kalibangan: Located in Rajasthan.
  • Great Bath: Found at Mohenjo-daro.
  • Script: The Indus script remains undeciphered.
Exam Point: The Great Bath is located at Mohenjo-daro.

2. Vedic Period

The Vedic period is associated with the composition of the Vedic texts and the development of early Vedic society.

  • Rigveda: Oldest of the four Vedas.
  • Samaveda: Mainly associated with melodies and chants.
  • Yajurveda: Contains sacrificial formulas.
  • Atharvaveda: Contains hymns, spells and prayers.
  • Early Vedic society was mainly pastoral.
  • Agriculture became increasingly important in the later Vedic period.
Exam Point: Rigveda is the oldest Veda.

3. Jainism

Jainism is an ancient Indian religious tradition. Mahavira was the 24th Tirthankara and played a major role in the development of Jainism.

  • Mahavira: 24th Tirthankara.
  • Mahavira was born at Kundagrama near Vaishali according to traditional accounts.
  • Important principles include Ahimsa, Satya and Aparigraha.
  • Jainism strongly emphasizes non-violence and self-discipline.
Exam Point: Mahavira was the 24th Tirthankara of Jainism.

4. Buddhism

Gautama Buddha founded Buddhism. His teachings focused on overcoming suffering and achieving liberation.

  • Birth name: Siddhartha Gautama.
  • Enlightenment: Bodh Gaya.
  • First Sermon: Sarnath.
  • Mahaparinirvana: Kushinagar.
  • Four Noble Truths: Central teachings of Buddhism.
  • Eightfold Path: Path leading towards the end of suffering.
Exam Point: Buddha delivered his first sermon at Sarnath.

5. Mahajanapadas

Around the 6th century BCE, sixteen major states known as the Mahajanapadas existed in northern India.

  • Anga
  • Magadha
  • Kashi
  • Kosala
  • Vajji
  • Malla
  • Chedi
  • Vatsa
  • Kuru
  • Panchala
  • Matsya
  • Surasena
  • Assaka
  • Avanti
  • Gandhara
  • Kamboja
Exam Point: Magadha became one of the most powerful Mahajanapadas.

6. Mauryan Empire

The Mauryan Empire was founded by Chandragupta Maurya. It became one of the largest empires in ancient India.

  • Founder: Chandragupta Maurya.
  • Advisor: Kautilya (Chanakya).
  • Capital: Pataliputra.
  • Arthashastra: Traditionally attributed to Kautilya.
  • Chandragupta Maurya was succeeded by Bindusara.
  • Ashoka was the grandson of Chandragupta Maurya.

7. Ashoka

Ashoka was one of the most famous rulers of the Mauryan Empire. His reign is especially remembered for the Kalinga War and his later support for Buddhism.

  • Kalinga War: Generally dated to around 261 BCE.
  • The war caused Ashoka to adopt a policy strongly influenced by Dhamma.
  • Ashoka supported the spread of Buddhism.
  • His inscriptions were written on rocks and pillars.
  • The Lion Capital of Sarnath is associated with Ashoka.
Exam Point: Ashoka's inscriptions are an important source for Mauryan history.

8. Gupta Empire

The Gupta period is often associated with major developments in literature, mathematics, astronomy, art and architecture.

  • Important ruler: Chandragupta I.
  • Samudragupta: Known for his military campaigns.
  • Chandragupta II: Also known as Vikramaditya.
  • Kalidasa: Famous Sanskrit poet and dramatist.
  • Aryabhata: Famous mathematician and astronomer.
  • Fa-Hien visited India during the reign of Chandragupta II.
Exam Point: Samudragupta is known from the Allahabad Pillar inscription.

9. Sangam Age

The Sangam period is associated with early Tamil literature and the history of the southern Indian kingdoms.

  • Chera: Important ancient Tamil kingdom.
  • Chola: Important southern dynasty.
  • Pandya: Important ancient Tamil kingdom.
  • Sangam literature provides information about early South Indian society and culture.

10. Ancient Indian Literature

  • Vedas: Rigveda, Samaveda, Yajurveda and Atharvaveda.
  • Upanishads: Philosophical texts associated with Vedic tradition.
  • Ramayana: Traditionally attributed to Valmiki.
  • Mahabharata: Traditionally attributed to Vyasa.
  • Arthashastra: Traditionally attributed to Kautilya.
  • Abhijnanasakuntalam: Associated with Kalidasa.

11. Ancient Indian Science

  • Aryabhata: Important mathematician and astronomer.
  • Zero: Indian mathematicians made major contributions to the development of the decimal place-value system.
  • Sushruta: Traditionally associated with ancient Indian surgery.
  • Charaka: Traditionally associated with ancient Indian medicine.

12. Important Ancient Monuments and Sites

  • Sanchi Stupa: Important Buddhist monument.
  • Ajanta Caves: Famous for Buddhist paintings and sculptures.
  • Ellora Caves: Famous for Buddhist, Hindu and Jain monuments.
  • Nalanda: Famous ancient centre of learning.
  • Sarnath: Associated with Buddha's first sermon.
  • Bodh Gaya: Associated with Buddha's enlightenment.

13. Important Ancient History Dates

  • c. 2600–1900 BCE - Mature phase of Indus Valley Civilization
  • 6th century BCE - Rise of Mahajanapadas
  • c. 563 BCE - Traditional date for Buddha's birth
  • c. 599 BCE - Traditional Jain date for Mahavira's birth
  • c. 322 BCE - Rise of Mauryan Empire
  • c. 261 BCE - Kalinga War
  • 4th–5th century CE - Major Gupta period
